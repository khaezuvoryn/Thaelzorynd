# Thaelzorynd

> A book grew teeth. The librarian began turning its pages with a silver spoon.

## The Library beneath the Evening Star

Thaelzorynd opens beneath a star that appears before sunset. Its entrance is a narrow arch standing alone in a field of dry reeds. Visitors walk beneath it and emerge inside a library where the windows show no field, only a sky filled with slow white sparks.

The shelves are made of bone polished smooth by hands that left no fingerprints. Each holds books bound in different materials: soft stone, dark silk, transparent wood, a substance that resembles snow until touched.

At the far end sits a librarian with a silver spoon beside his desk. His ledger lists the names of everyone who has entered. Its final entry is always written in the handwriting of the next visitor.

## The Book That Refused Its Cover

The tooth-bearing book was found between two volumes whose titles had been erased. Its cover was dark and warm, with a shallow mark resembling a closed eye. Its pages moved whenever a nearby reader whispered.

The librarian opened it using the spoon. Along the edges of the paper grew small white teeth, arranged in pairs that did not meet when the book was closed.

Inside, the text described a creature sleeping around a tower of shelves. Its scales bore lines of writing. Its wings sheltered a desk at which someone was attempting to open a dark book.

The librarian stopped reading. A tooth fell onto the spoon and became a letter he could recognize but could not pronounce. He placed it inside his ledger. The next visitor's name acquired one additional syllable.

## The Reader of Transparent Wood

A reader selected a volume bound in transparent wood. Through its cover, she could see a small room containing a chair, a lamp, and a window facing the evening star.

The volume's words described her journey to the arch. They described the reeds, the librarian, and her hand resting upon the cover. The following page described her leaving the library with a silver spoon in her pocket.

She closed the book before reaching that passage. Across the room, the librarian's spoon disappeared from the desk. The reader searched her pockets and found a small white tooth instead.

She returned the tooth. The librarian thanked her for bringing back the missing hour. Outside the windows, one spark stopped moving and began to shine like a star seen from much farther away.

## The Shelves beyond the Ledger

The ledger's pages contain narrow spaces between names. In those spaces, a visitor can sometimes see miniature shelves extending into a distance that the paper should not contain.

A scholar examined them through a lens. He found books bearing names absent from the ledger. Their covers were closed, but their shadows were open upon small desks where no readers sat.

He attempted to copy a name. The lens clouded over, showing the pupil of an enormous eye. Behind its reflection, the scholar saw the tower described in the tooth-bearing book, with the sleeping creature curled around its base.

The scholar lowered the lens. His copy contained a drawing of the arch. Beneath it stood a figure carrying a book large enough to shelter the entire library beneath its cover.

## The Language beneath the Tongue

**Thael — zor — ynd — ekha.**

These sounds appear along the tooth-bearing book's spine when the evening star is reflected in a window. The librarian reads them silently, moving the spoon from one side of the desk to the other.

An apprentice tried speaking the first sound. The shelves leaned toward him. He spoke the second, and every closed book cast the shadow of a wing. Before he could continue, the librarian placed the spoon against his lips.

The apprentice heard a sentence inside the metal. It asked him to name the creature before the creature named the room.

He did not answer. For the rest of that evening, the ledger's final entry remained blank. The windows showed a field of reeds beneath an unfamiliar moon.

## A Conversation Recorded without Ink

> Why does the book need teeth?
>
> To hold the word its cover cannot remember.
>
> Who writes the next visitor's name?
>
> The hand that has not passed beneath the arch.
>
> What waits beyond the shelves inside the page?
>
> The room that was read before it was built.

The apprentice recorded these answers by pressing the spoon against a blank sheet. No mark appeared, but anyone holding the page could hear the exchange through the bones of their fingers.

The librarian placed the sheet between the two erased titles. Their covers became warm. By morning, one title had returned, though its letters resembled teeth viewed through clear wood.

## The Star That Arrives before Evening

The library continues opening beneath the arch. Its visitors bring no lamps; they read by the white sparks beyond the windows and leave before the final name begins to darken in the ledger.

The librarian keeps the spoon nearby. The tooth-bearing book remains closed, except when its shadow turns a page upon the floor. Sometimes that page shows a tower. Sometimes it shows a field where a sleeping dragon has folded its wings around a narrow arch.

Thaelzorynd waits beneath the early star. Between two names in the ledger, an empty desk has acquired a silver spoon and a book whose first tooth is beginning to grow.
